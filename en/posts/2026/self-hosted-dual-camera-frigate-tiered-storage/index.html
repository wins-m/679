Self-Hosted Dual-Camera Recording with Frigate and Two NAS Devices

26.10.28 6-minute read
WM
Technology
NAS • Docker • Frigate • Self-Hosted

I installed two inexpensive indoor cameras to monitor a pet while away from home. The requirements were straightforward: remote live viewing, timeline playback, roughly one month of retention, and no recurring cloud-recording or AI-recognition subscription.

I already had two NAS devices. One runs fnOS and Docker; the other, a Synology, has disk space suitable for archived video. The interesting challenge became: can the first device handle ingestion and recent recordings, the second keep older footage, and Frigate still present a single playback timeline?

As of October 28, 2026, both cameras are recording, and the essential migration and playback paths have been tested. This is a record of the actual design and its limitations, not a copy-and-paste installation guide.

1. Choose cameras for protocols, not subscriptions Link to heading

I bought two TP-LINK TL-IPC45CL-V2 units for RMB 120 each. They offer infrared night vision, pan/tilt, Wi-Fi, H.265, and RTSP output. I do not need the vendor’s cloud storage or recognition subscription. Reliable access to the video streams matters much more.

StreamActual configurationPurpose
MainH.265 / 2880×1620Continuous recording, high-resolution playback
SubH.264 / 640×360Low-bandwidth previews and detection

The deployed setup also preserves AAC audio. Indoor cameras call for careful consideration of recording scope, consent, and access permissions.

2. One Frigate instance, two physical storage tiers Link to heading

                   Camera A + Camera B
                            | RTSP
                            v
                      fnOS + Docker
                      Frigate 0.18.2
                     /            \
                go2rtc        OpenVINO CPU
             live restream    cat/person detection
                     \            /
                      /media/frigate
                         mergerfs
                       /          \
                  fnOS local     Synology SMB
                  RW: new       NC: archived
                       \          /
                    One playback timeline

Frigate handles recording, indexes, retention and playback. go2rtc restreams live video and makes it practical to use separate camera streams for high-quality recording and light-weight detection. OpenVINO on the CPU tracks cat and person using a bundled model. mergerfs exposes a unified media directory over the local staging area and the remote archive.

The Frigate configuration and SQLite database remain on a local filesystem, not on SMB or mergerfs. Video is tiered; metadata and frequently updated state stay local.

3. Record the main stream, detect on the substream Link to heading

Both cameras use the same arrangement: continuous high-resolution recording from their main streams, with detection at 640×360 and 5 fps on the substreams.

This sanitized excerpt illustrates the camera setup. Replace all placeholders with your own values. It is not the complete production configuration.

record:
  enabled: true
  continuous:
    days: 30

go2rtc:
  streams:
    camera_a:
      - "rtsp://<USER>:<URL_ENCODED_PASSWORD>@<CAMERA_A_IP>:554/stream1#backchannel=0"
    camera_a_sub:
      - "rtsp://<USER>:<URL_ENCODED_PASSWORD>@<CAMERA_A_IP>:554/stream2#backchannel=0"
    camera_b:
      - "rtsp://<USER>:<URL_ENCODED_PASSWORD>@<CAMERA_B_IP>:554/stream1#backchannel=0"
    camera_b_sub:
      - "rtsp://<USER>:<URL_ENCODED_PASSWORD>@<CAMERA_B_IP>:554/stream2#backchannel=0"

cameras:
  camera_a:
    ffmpeg:
      inputs:
        - path: "rtsp://<USER>:<URL_ENCODED_PASSWORD>@<CAMERA_A_IP>:554/stream1"
          roles: [record]
        - path: "rtsp://127.0.0.1:8554/camera_a_sub"
          input_args: preset-rtsp-restream
          roles: [detect]
    detect:
      enabled: true
      width: 640
      height: 360
      fps: 5
    objects:
      track: [cat, person]

  camera_b:
    ffmpeg:
      inputs:
        - path: "rtsp://<USER>:<URL_ENCODED_PASSWORD>@<CAMERA_B_IP>:554/stream1"
          roles: [record]
        - path: "rtsp://127.0.0.1:8554/camera_b_sub"
          input_args: preset-rtsp-restream
          roles: [detect]
    detect:
      enabled: true
      width: 640
      height: 360
      fps: 5
    objects:
      track: [cat, person]

The real deployment also configures the OpenVINO model, audio recording, authentication and per-camera permissions. Do not publish working RTSP credentials.

4. Why not record directly to Synology? Link to heading

The first iteration bound the Synology SMB share directly to Frigate’s /media/frigate. Simple, but every new recording then depended on the network mount.

The revised design writes new files to local fnOS staging, periodically copies finished recordings to Synology, and keeps the same logical media paths throughout.

In mergerfs terms:

Local staging = RW (create new files here)
Synology      = NC (read/delete existing files; no new creation)
                       |
                Unified media view
                       |
             Frigate /media/frigate

The migration preserves each segment’s relative path, including its date, hour, camera identifier, and segment name. Frigate’s indexed path stays the same. mergerfs resolves the file from whichever branch currently holds it.

Consequently, recent and older recordings appear on one timeline, even though their physical locations differ. But moving the file off the local tier does not create a backup copy. Tiering is neither backup nor high availability.

5. Migration after 24 hours—or under storage pressure Link to heading

The current rules are:

SettingValue
Continuous retention30 days from recording time
Age-based migrationSegments ended over 24 hours ago
Shared high watermark100 GiB of local staging content
Pressure-relief targetAt or below 80 GiB
Segment settle windowEnd time and file mtime at least 5 minutes old
Migration pollingAround 5 minutes after the previous run finishes
Free-space guardStop recording below 20 GiB local free space

The 100 GiB figure is a migration trigger, not a hard quota, and is shared across both cameras. Under pressure, settled files younger than 24 hours may be migrated, oldest first. Other local staging content such as screenshots also contributes to this size.

To reduce the chance of losing a file halfway through migration, the workflow:

  1. Checks that Frigate has indexed the finished segment and that it is stable.
  2. Verifies the source and remote mount identity.
  3. Copies to a temporary remote file and syncs it.
  4. Reads back the remote file and compares its SHA256 with the source.
  5. Checks that source identity and database reference still match.
  6. Publishes the remote file and removes the local source.

Conflicting remote contents are not silently overwritten. A separate migration journal supports retries and reconciliation with Frigate’s concurrent retention cleanup. The file operation and SQLite metadata are not a single atomic transaction, so this is not a claim of transactional distributed storage.

6. Validate playback—not merely file existence Link to heading

When adding camera B, I reused the existing Frigate deployment instead of creating another container or rebuilding the database. I added its camera definition and go2rtc streams.

The following were verified:

  • Both cameras stream and record concurrently, with independent local directories.
  • Local and archived footage can be played back through Frigate’s existing endpoints.
  • A selected camera B segment was dry-run, migrated, SHA256-checked and processed again idempotently, with its database path and timestamps unchanged.
  • 19,013 pre-existing camera A recording entries were compared before and after the change, with no missing entries or modified paths/times.
  • Sample playback media passed audio/video decoding checks.

These tests support basic operational correctness, not universal fault tolerance. I have not performed an actual NAS power-cycle or SMB-disconnection drill, nor filled the staging area to test the 100 GiB trigger. SMB stalls could still affect mergerfs I/O. When Synology is offline, archived footage is unavailable.

7. Privacy and the real operating cost Link to heading

No vendor cloud-recording subscription is needed for this setup. Video storage and object detection run locally, though not subscribing to cloud storage does not imply the cameras make no outbound connections. Further network isolation requires its own testing.

Indoor recording requires proper access controls and appropriate consent. I am deliberately omitting my device addresses, remote endpoints, account names, SSH access, and real filesystem paths.

At an illustrative average of 2 Mbps per camera, two continuously recording cameras generate around 43.2 GB per day, or roughly 1.3 TB over 30 days before other overheads. Real usage varies with bitrate, audio and recording policy. Existing hardware reduces upfront spending, but drives, electricity and maintenance are not free.

This is not the simplest solution for someone starting from scratch. For a home setup that already has NAS capacity, however, it combines inexpensive RTSP cameras, local detection, and two storage devices while preserving one playback interface.

The most interesting part was not choosing the cameras. It was migrating recordings across storage tiers without invalidating Frigate’s index—and being precise about how far that design has actually been tested.
